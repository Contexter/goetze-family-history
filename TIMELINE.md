# A provisional family timeline

The timeline combines documented episodes and research leads. Citations refer to [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). A date in this list does **not** establish a complete chain of kinship.

| Period | What is known, and what remains open |
| --- | --- |
| Early 1800s–1856 | The Glauchau textile firm later known as **Götze senior und Söhne** developed from an earlier business. Its 1856 description names **Carl Eduard** and **Emil Herrmann Götze** as owners; it refers to textile production, finishing, outworkers, and distant markets. See S1. |
| 1849 and 1857 | Marriage records discussed in the *Hessische Biografie* connect the Glauchau entrepreneurs to merchant families in **Frankfurt am Main**, including **Wurster** and **Brunner**. The original registers should still be checked. See S3. |
| 23 September 1863 | **Rudolf Emil Fritz Götze** was born in Glauchau. His parents **Emil Hermann Götze** and **Albertine née Brunner** are now independently named by the original 1892 civil marriage register; the 1863 baptismal entry remains to be read. See S4 and S12. |
| 1884–1893 | Rudolf studied medicine and published an 1888 lecture on **Heinrich von Stein, Wagner, and Schopenhauer** and an 1893 work on **lead poisoning**. His reasons for choosing these subjects are not documented. See S5. |
| 8 April 1892 | **Original civil marriage register No. 228, Dresden:** Rudolf Götze married **Else Römmler** (*7 May 1868, Dresden); both fathers witnessed and signed the entry. The bride's father was photographer **Carl Emil Römmler**. See S12. |
| 1893–1894 | **Dr. Rudolf Götze in Würzburg** participated in a coordinated, antisemitic protest against Jewish merchants Emil and Samuel Schmul adopting the name Goetze. The 1893 letter and 1894 newspaper reports are available in digital form. See S6–S7. |
| 1897–1899 | Secondary biographical accounts place the births of **Albrecht** in Leipzig (1897), **Gottfried** in Naunhof (1898), and **Raimund** in Naunhof (1899). Original birth entries and a complete list of the children remain outstanding. See S4 and S10. |
| 30 October 1920 | Rudolf died in **Alzey**, according to biographical records. His medical cause of death has not been established from a contemporary official source. See S4. |
| 1933–1940 | The Berlin and exile histories of other people named Goetze remain under investigation. Published 1937 autobiographical writing and exile-archive catalogues attest to **Gertraute Goetze-Fischer** in New York; her relationship to Rudolf's immediate family still needs direct documentation. See S8. |
| 1938–1939 | In the separate Schmul–Goetze naming controversy, Nazi authorities pressured members of the **Jewish Goetze family** to resume the surname Schmul, as discussed by historian Jane Caplan. Do not merge this family with Rudolf's line without genealogical proof. See S9. |
| 14 December 1989 | A California death-index transcription records **Gutrune Falckon**, born 8 June 1903, with paternal surname **Goetze** and mother's maiden surname **Roemmler**. This is a strong family lead; an official certificate is needed. See S11. |

We aim to turn each entry into an auditable link to an original source, and we welcome corrections to dates, spellings, and relationships.
